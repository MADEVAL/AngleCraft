# Example: Fitness Coach (Health-Adjacent, Educational)

**Input:** "I am a fitness coach. People struggle to stay consistent."

**Mode:** Quick
**Safety:** Educational framing only. No diagnosis, treatment, or outcome promises. Recommend a qualified professional for health conditions.

| # | Angle | Hook | Direction |
|---|-------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Level Error | "You do not have a motivation problem. You have a friction problem." | Shift from willpower to removing logistical barriers. |
| 2 | Price of Solution | "Yes, 5 a.m. workouts work. The price is your evening." | Show the sleep and schedule trade-off honestly. |
| 3 | Conditions | "If you train before work, prepare your kit the night before. If after work, book a fixed slot." | Give a decision rule based on schedule, not discipline. |
| 4 | Myth vs Reality | "Everyone says consistency means daily. It actually means never missing twice." | Replace the all-or-nothing myth with a recovery rule. |
| 5 | Paradox | "The more you track, the less you want to train." | Explore when measurement becomes avoidance. |

**Why this mix:** behavior and scheduling, not medical claims. The set avoids diagnosis, treatment promises, spot-reduction claims, and any "cure" framing.

`If the final content touches injury, pain, or medical conditions, add a "not medical advice" disclaimer and route the reader to a qualified professional.`
