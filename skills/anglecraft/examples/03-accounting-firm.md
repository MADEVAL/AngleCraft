# Example: Small Accounting Firm (Professional Service)

**Input:** "I run a small accounting firm. Clients say we're too expensive."

**Mode:** Quick

| # | Angle | Hook | Direction |
|---|-------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Level Error | "Clients do not think you are expensive. They think you are interchangeable." | Reframe the price objection as a positioning problem. |
| 2 | Price of Solution | "Yes, a cheaper accountant saves [$X] a year. The bill arrives at tax season." | Compare the fee against the cost of errors and rework. |
| 3 | Conditions | "If your books are clean, fixed fee. If they are a mess, cleanup project first." | Segment prospects by bookkeeping state. |
| 4 | Myth vs Reality | "Everyone says clients want the lowest price. They actually want the fewest surprises." | Replace the price myth with a predictability story. |
| 5 | Contrary Thesis | "Stop selling hours. Sell the outcome you are accountable for." | Make the case for outcome-based pricing. |

`Replace bracketed placeholders with verified data before publishing.`

**Why this mix:** the core objection is price, so the set combines a reframe (Level Error), an honest cost comparison (Price), a self-selection rule (Conditions), and a pricing-model argument (Contrary). Avoids generic "value" language.
